Towards a “perfect assay description”
Bioactivity data types in ChEMBL have diversified over time [1], but in general the two main classes of documents containing the information are of type “PUBLICATION” (extracted from the scientific literature) and “DATASET” (provided directly by depositors). Whereas we continue to work closely with depositors to ensure the submitted assay information meets our minimum criteria and follows our general structure for data deposition, for literature sources, the submitted data are more uniform in general. This allows us to use a high-level template for constructing the assay DESCRIPTION for literature data, which consists of the following components:

Activity of drug + Target/Biological system + Expression system
+ Inducing agent + Assessment + Dose + Dose unit + Route
+ Drug regimen + Incubation time + Method + Miscellaneous data

However, the “perfect assay description” will vary depending on the nature of the assay (i.e., ASSAY_TYPE). For example, a precise record of a purified protein target (source organism, amino acid changes, and deletions) is critical for binding assays, whereas the route of drug administration is more relevant to pharmacokinetics studies (ADME assays). Furthermore, the parameters necessary for interpretation vary according to the specific assay; a record of the solvent, pH and temperature (but not necessarily a target) is essential for drug stability assays, whereas information on drug metabolites is important for metabolism assays, and the target tissue (as well as biological system) may be key to understanding toxicity assays. For certain functional and binding assays, specific parameters, such as the voltage protocol for electrophysiology assays with ion channel targets such as hERG, improve interpretation [30].
