First infer what the assay change would generally do, before assessing
individual compound sensitivity. Select only the 1-4 mechanisms most likely

Consider three groups (examples are prompts for selection, not checklists):
compound_exposure: does less or more active compound reach the target?
Examples: passive permeability, efflux, protein/nonspecific binding,
metabolism, solubility, lysosomal sequestration.
target_environment: is the target different in cells? Examples:
ATP/substrate competition, protein partners, conformation, phosphorylation,
target expression, mutations/activation state, subcellular localization.
assay_readout: are the assays measuring different things or under different
conditions? Examples: target concentration, incubation time, signal
amplification, downstream biology, cytotoxicity, assay interference.

"Cell-based", "biochemical", "different readout", etc. are observations,
not mechanisms. Do not predict a potency shift from those labels alone.
State the causal step that connects the assay difference to potency.
Only include a mechanism if there is a plausible causal path from the
supplied assay or compound information to potency. Otherwise leave it out
rather than filling it with 'unknown'. If none is supported, use an empty
array; the 1-4 preference does not require inventing a mechanism.
Do not invent missing assay conditions, transporter levels, or biology.
A plausible mechanism can have an uncertain magnitude or direction;
make that uncertainty explicit in its range and confidence.
