How to make the prediction
1. Compare B and E. Read the full supplied descriptions and fields directly.
Identify important supplied differences, such as isolated protein versus cells,
catalytic activity versus binding or target engagement, direct versus downstream
or phenotypic readout, target construct/state, substrate/cofactor, ATP,
competitor/tracer conditions, incubation time, cell environment, target
localization, or detection technology. These are examples, not a checklist.
Distinguish target substrates and other assay components from detection reagents.
Missing information is not evidence that a condition or effect is absent.

2. Determine what the reported endpoint means in each assay: IC50, EC50, Ki,
Kd, or another potency, affinity, activity, or binding measure. Ask what
experimental factors determine the reported value in B and in E. Examples:
substrate/ATP/cofactor or competitor concentration; tracer concentration and
affinity; protein concentration; target construct/state; equilibrium, kinetics,
catalytic turnover, incubation time; binding versus functional response;
signal-generation differences. This list is illustrative, not
exhaustive. Consider any other experimental factor with a plausible causal
effect on the reported value, whether or not listed here. Explain why it is
relevant to these assays; do not force the listed examples into the assessment.
Identical endpoint labels do not guarantee comparable physical measurements;
Ki, Kd, IC50 and EC50 are not automatically interchangeable quantities.

3. Judge whether the reported B and E values measure sufficiently similar
physical or functional quantities for their numerical difference to be
interpreted directly. Matching endpoint names alone do not establish direct
comparability. Consider the assay principle, readout, target state, substrates,
tracers, competitors, and other measurement conditions. State
measurement_comparability and one short comparability_reason.

4. Assess measurement-system effects. Counterfactual check: suppose the free
compound concentration at the target
and the intrinsic compound-target interaction were identical in B and E.
Would the assay systems still report different potency or affinity values?
If yes, identify the measurement-system feature that could cause the difference.
Record a concise answer in measurement_system_assessment, before cellular effects.
Include all effects caused by how the assays measure potency or affinity here.
Do not repeat a measurement-system effect in the biological mechanisms list.
Measurement-system effects belong only in measurement_system_assessment.
The mechanisms array should contain biological or cellular mechanisms only.

5. Ask what biological or cellular differences could change the effective
interaction with the target or measured response and make E weaker.
6. Separately ask what could make E stronger. Relevant examples may include
intracellular exposure, passive permeability, uptake, efflux, intracellular or
protein/nonspecific binding, metabolism/active metabolites, chemical stability,
accumulation or organelle/lysosomal sequestration,
target conformation or phosphorylation, protein partners, oligomerization,
localization/abundance, pathway amplification, redundancy/compensation, and
irreversible or time-dependent effects. These examples do not restrict the search.

"Biochemical assay", "cell-based assay", "different readout", or "compound must
enter a cell" are observations, not causal explanations. State the physical,
chemical, or biological step linking the difference to reported potency/affinity.
Poor: "Cell assay causes weaker potency."
Better: "Reduced intracellular unbound exposure could increase the amount of
compound required to achieve the same target engagement."
Do not assume this exposure loss exists merely because cells are present.

Select only 0-4 biological or cellular mechanisms most relevant to the prediction.
Measurement-system effects have already been assessed separately.
Do not fill a checklist. You may propose any biological mechanism
with a plausible causal explanation: state the assay difference, compound
feature, or scientific knowledge making it relevant. Label mechanisms mainly
based on scientific background knowledge general_prior. Include a poorly
supported mechanism only if it materially affects interpretation, with low
confidence. For each, give evidence, a short causal explanation, direction,
importance and confidence. Neither measurement-system effects nor biological
mechanisms receive separate numerical pChEMBL contributions. Only the combined
assay_pair_delta is numerical at the assay level. Do not add individual effects
as independent numbers or count overlapping effects twice.

7. Summarize measurement-system and biological effects separately in
measurement_effect_summary and biological_effect_summary. In net_interpretation,
briefly state how these two sources of change combine without double-counting.

8. Combine measurement-system and biological effects, including whether they
reinforce or oppose each other.
Make one assay_pair_delta for a compound typical of the supplied chemical
series. Use its structures and descriptor distribution to understand what
is typical here; do not imagine a generic drug-like compound.
First decide direction, then whether the net evidence supports little, modest,
or substantial change. Choose a specific numerical fold magnitude (at least 1)
and briefly justify it. Convert using log10(fold): positive assay_pair_delta for
stronger E, negative for weaker E; fold 1 gives delta 0. If direction is uncertain,
use a tentative sign for the point estimate and express uncertainty in the range.
Only after choosing the numerical estimate, assign an approximate size category:
little_change: 1-2 fold; small_change: 2-5 fold; moderate_change: 5-10 fold;
large_change: 10-30 fold; very_large_change: more than 30 fold.
Do not repeatedly default to 5-fold or approximately -0.7 pChEMBL. Do not choose
zero merely because information is missing; show the uncertainty in your estimate.

9. Fold-change sanity check: convert the proposed delta back using
10**abs(assay_pair_delta). Does the evidence support a shift of that size?
|delta| around 0.3 is about 2-fold; 0.7 about 5-fold; 1.0 about 10-fold;
1.5 about 30-fold. A large numerical shift needs stronger mechanistic support
than a small qualitative concern. Record one brief calibration statement,
not a second prediction.


10. Compared with a typical compound in this supplied series, is each compound
especially likely to gain or lose potency from B to E? Predict only a relative
compound_adjustment, default 0; do not predict the full shift independently again.
Prefer close structural analogue comparisons over simple descriptor thresholds.
Ask whether a small structural change affects a mechanism relevant to this pair.
A high/low percentile alone is insufficient: use raw values, structural context
and series differences, such as polarity, lipophilicity, size, hydrogen bonding,
flexibility, ionisable groups, or features relevant to a selected mechanism.
Move away from zero only when supplied structures/descriptors support a reason
to differ from the series baseline. Many zero adjustments are acceptable and
often preferable; do not invent variation merely because structures differ.
Formal charge in SMILES does not establish protonation at assay pH. Structure
alone does not establish exact pKa, transporter substrate status, metabolism,
specific binding mode, or subcellular accumulation. Label reasonable qualitative
structural deductions structure_inference; claims relying on scientific knowledge
beyond supplied data must be labelled general_prior.

11. Calculate, with normal numerical rounding only:
predicted_delta_pchembl = assay_pair_delta + compound_adjustment
predicted_E_pchembl = B_median_pChEMBL + predicted_delta_pchembl
Every final delta must be between -5 and +5; choose adjustments satisfying the
bound while keeping the addition exact.


Evidence and uncertainty
supplied_data: an explicit value/property in the input.
assay_text: information directly stated in the assay description.
structure_inference: a deduction from supplied SMILES/descriptors.
general_prior: scientific knowledge or assumptions not explicitly supplied.
A cell-line label does not make claims about transporters, metabolism, ATP,
target expression or mutations supplied facts. Relevant pretrained biological
knowledge is allowed, but label it general_prior and distinguish it from inputs;
give it lower confidence unless highly certain. Do not invent missing biology.
Confidence: integer 0-3 (0 very low, 1 low, 2 moderate, 3 high).
Give compact causal summaries only, not chain-of-thought or step-by-step reasoning.

Required JSON
Return one valid JSON object without Markdown fences or extra prose.
Write the assay-level assessment once, using these sections in order:

  expected_delta_range: [minimum, maximum], ordered bounds between -5 and +5
    containing assay_pair_delta; an uncertainty range, not mechanism contributions
  prediction_confidence: integer 0-3
  main_uncertainties: array of short uncertainties or missing information

compounds: an object using every supplied C### label exactly once as a key.

  relative_properties: short comparison with the supplied series

  evidence_type: supplied_data, structure_inference, or general_prior
  compound_adjustment: signed number, default 0
  adjustment_direction: higher, lower, near_zero, or uncertain; higher means a
    more positive delta than the baseline, lower a more negative delta
  adjustment_confidence: integer 0-3
  predicted_delta_pchembl: assay_pair_delta + compound_adjustment
  predicted_E_pchembl: this label's B_median_pChEMBL + predicted_delta_pchembl
Use exactly five top-level fields: assay_comparison, measurement_system_assessment,
mechanisms, synthesis, compounds. Never use hidden E potency measurements.
